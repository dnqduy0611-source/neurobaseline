What is inside

One companion for the person, one workspace for the clinician.

Everything below is built and running in this prototype.

01

Four short cognitive tasks

Reaction time, tap-and-stop (Go/No-Go), symbol matching and picture memory. About five minutes on a phone, with practice trials and a check for difficulty seeing or tapping.

02

A personal baseline

Five sessions in two weeks record the person's usual level. After that, one session a month is compared with that level using a Reliable Change Index and a trend test. Every status lists which measure moved and against which limit.

03

Daily habits check-in

Four questions a day on sleep, movement and time with others, about 30 seconds. A four-week chart sits next to the session results, and each week is compared with the person's own earlier weeks.

04

Risk profile

An optional profile built on the 14 modifiable risk factors of the 2024 Lancet Commission, such as hearing, blood pressure, physical activity and social contact. Each factor present comes with one thing to act on.

05

Optional brain-rhythm check

A resting EEG recording from a consumer headband is processed in the browser. The theta/alpha ratio and alpha frequency are compared with the person's own earlier recordings.

06

Insights and a plan

One page reads session results, daily habits, the risk profile and the EEG check together, says how they fit, and gives at most three steps for the next two weeks. Every line comes from a fixed rule and states why it is there.

How it works

From a first session to a decision a clinician can check.

  1. Record the usual levelFive sessions over two weeks. The first is a warm-up; the next four set the personal baseline for each task.
  2. Check in once a monthThe same five minutes, on the same phone. Each session is compared with the person's own baseline, never with a population cut-off.
  3. Act on a confirmed changeA single unusual result asks for a repeat in two weeks. Only a change that holds, or a steady downward trend, advises clinical review.

For clinicians

Who to see first, why they were flagged, and what to rule out.

A priority list, a summary across sources for each person, the results against the baseline, the daily habits the person chose to share, a checklist of 12 reversible contributors prompted by the person's own answers, and a review record. Files are imported by hand; nothing is uploaded.

Evidence

Why a personal baseline, in three numbers.

All three analyses are in the repository with their code, and can be rerun.

40% vs 0.7%

One fixed cut-off that flags 5% of people with no memory complaint flags 40% of those who did not finish 9th grade and 0.7% of college graduates. NHANES 2011–2014, 3,011 adults aged 60 and over.

76% vs 10%

At the same 5% of false referrals over three years, a trend test on the personal baseline finds 76% of slowly declining people within 24 months; a fixed cut-off finds 10%. The rule in the app finds 80% at 5.8%. Simulated data.

0.87 AUC

The theta/alpha ratio of resting EEG separates Alzheimer's disease from healthy controls in a public dataset of 88 people, and keeps 0.87–0.88 when reduced to headband electrode positions.

Safeguards and limits

Careful about what it says, and about what it does not know yet.

Safeguards

  • No diagnosis. The app reports a change from a person's own level. A clinician decides what it means.
  • Data stay on the device. No account and no server. Health answers, daily habits and EEG results leave the phone only when the person ticks the box and exports a file.
  • A clear referral path. Red-flag symptoms lead straight to urgent-care advice; a confirmed change leads to a summary for a doctor.
  • Explained, not scored. Every flag and every step of the plan states its reason. Fixed rules choose the words; no AI model writes them.
  • Simulated data are labelled wherever they appear.

Limits

  • No data from real participants yet. Thresholds come from simulation and published values and are marked as assumptions in the code.
  • Self-report with single questions stands in for the instruments used in the risk-factor studies. Daily habits are recorded and shown; they never change the status.
  • No headband connection. EEG is read from a recording file; no device has been tested.
  • Not a medical device. A pilot with volunteers and clinical validation come before any use in care.
